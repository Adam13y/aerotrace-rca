Title: Starship Integrated Flight Test 2 - Super Heavy booster loss during boostback
Date: 18 November 2023
Vehicle: SpaceX Super Heavy booster (Starship IFT-2)
Sources: SpaceX Starship Flight Test 2 summary (2024)

FAILURE MODE
Loss of oxidizer turbopump inlet pressure on multiple Raptor engines during the boostback burn, followed by an energetic engine failure and loss of the booster.

CAUSAL CHAIN
All 33 booster engines operated through ascent and the vehicle completed hot-stage separation. Shortly after the booster began its boostback burn, several Raptor engines started shutting down, and one engine then failed energetically; the booster was lost roughly three and a half minutes into flight. SpaceX identified the most likely root cause as a filter blockage where liquid oxygen (LOX) is supplied to the engines. The blockage reduced LOX inlet pressure at the engines' oxidizer turbopumps. Insufficient pump inlet pressure is the classic precondition for cavitation, which degrades pump performance and can damage turbomachinery. The degraded engines shut down, and one failed in a way that destroyed the vehicle. The upper stage was lost later in the flight from a separate cause.

TELEMETRY SIGNATURES
- Falling oxidizer pump inlet pressure on several engines at the same time: a shared-feed symptom (tank, filter, ducting), not a single-engine fault.
- Engine shutdowns clustered in time during a manoeuvre that disturbs propellant settling (reorientation and boostback).
- Turbopump speed and vibration excursions on the affected engines.
- A rapid pressure and vibration transient immediately before loss of signal.

ROOT CAUSE
Blockage of the LOX supply filtration, causing low oxidizer turbopump inlet pressure and an engine failure. Reported corrective actions included hardware changes to improve propellant filtration in the booster oxidizer tanks and refined operations.

LESSON FOR ANOMALY ANALYSIS
When tank or inlet pressure falls first and turbopump vibration follows within seconds, treat the feed system as the initiating cause and the turbopump distress as a consequence (possible cavitation).
