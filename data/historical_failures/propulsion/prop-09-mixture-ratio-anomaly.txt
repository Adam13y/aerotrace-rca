Title: Off-nominal engine mixture ratio
Category: propulsion
Failure mode: Mixture ratio anomaly
Source type: synthetic
Case ID: prop-09-mixture-ratio-anomaly
Note: Synthetic engineering summary of a failure mode, not a record of a specific event.

FAILURE MODE
A mixture ratio anomaly is a departure of the oxidiser-to-fuel mass flow ratio from its design value. It affects specific impulse, combustion and turbine temperatures, and propellant utilisation. It can occur in the main chamber, gas generator or preburner; in staged-combustion engines an off-nominal preburner ratio can quickly drive turbine temperatures beyond limits.

CAUSAL CHAIN
A fault in one propellant circuit, such as a control-valve error, flow-sensor bias, regulator drift or a pressure change in one tank, alters that propellant's flow. The first observable is usually a divergence between oxidiser and fuel flow rates or inlet pressures, or a mixture-ratio control valve moving to an unusual position. Combustion temperature then changes: an oxidiser-rich shift raises temperatures and attacks hardware, while a fuel-rich shift lowers specific impulse and thrust. Turbine inlet temperatures follow within about a second. Over the burn one propellant depletes early, leaving residual in the other tank and risking premature shutdown.

TELEMETRY SIGNATURES
- Oxidiser and fuel flow or tank depletion rates diverge from the predicted ratio first
- Mixture-ratio control valve trends toward one end stop
- Turbine inlet temperature rises (oxidiser-rich), or chamber pressure and thrust fall (fuel-rich)
- One propellant low-level sensor triggers early near the end of the burn

ROOT CAUSE
Typical root causes include flow-meter calibration errors, incorrect calibration orifices, control-valve hysteresis or failure, pressurisation imbalance between tanks, density errors from off-nominal propellant temperature, and software faults in mixture-ratio control. Loading errors can produce an apparent anomaly late in flight.

HISTORICAL REFERENCE
No single public incident is cited for this summary; it describes a generic failure mode recognised in launch-vehicle engineering practice. It is typically revealed by post-flight propellant utilisation reconstruction or acceptance-test trending.

LESSONS LEARNED
- Estimate mixture ratio from independent measurements and compare them
- Bound control-valve authority so a single sensor fault cannot drive a hazardous ratio
- Account for propellant density variation in calibration and loading
