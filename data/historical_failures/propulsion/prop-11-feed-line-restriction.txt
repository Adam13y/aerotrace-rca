Title: Feed-line restriction causing a sustained chamber pressure drop
Category: propulsion
Failure mode: Feed-line restriction
Source type: synthetic
Case ID: prop-11-feed-line-restriction
Note: Synthetic engineering summary of a failure mode, not a record of a specific event.

FAILURE MODE
A feed-line restriction is a partial narrowing of the propellant flow path between the tank and the engine, from a partly closed or sticking valve, a kinked or collapsed flex line, a deformed bellows or a lodged foreign object. Flow is reduced but not stopped, and the pump still has enough inlet pressure margin to run without cavitating.

CAUSAL CHAIN
The restriction adds a fixed pressure loss in the feed line, so less propellant reaches the pump and injector. Chamber pressure falls over one to a few seconds to a new, lower level and stays there for as long as the restriction remains; it does not oscillate and does not recover by itself. Tank ullage pressures stay nominal because the tanks are not the problem. Because inlet pressure stays above the cavitation limit, turbopump vibration shows at most a short transient while the pump settles at the new operating point, with no sustained rise. The engine keeps running at reduced thrust.

TELEMETRY SIGNATURES
- Chamber pressure drops by several percent over 1-3 s and stays at the lower level (sustained, not oscillating)
- LOX and fuel tank pressures remain nominal; no pressurisation signature
- No sustained turbopump vibration rise; at most a brief transient as the pump settles
- Engine-bay temperature unaffected

ROOT CAUSE
Typical causes are a main or prevalve that did not fully open or partly closed in flight, a collapsed flex hose or bellows, ice in a cryogenic line after poor purging, or debris lodged at an elbow or orifice. Distinguish it from combustion instability, which oscillates, and from cavitation, which brings a sustained vibration rise and an inlet-pressure deficit.

HISTORICAL REFERENCE
No single public incident is cited for this summary; it describes a generic failure mode recognised in launch-vehicle engineering practice for sustained thrust shortfalls with nominal tank pressures.

LESSONS LEARNED
- Monitor valve position indicators alongside chamber pressure to separate valve and line restrictions
- Instrument pump inlet pressure so feed-line pressure loss can be computed directly
- Inspect flex lines and bellows for collapse after pressure reversals in ground testing
