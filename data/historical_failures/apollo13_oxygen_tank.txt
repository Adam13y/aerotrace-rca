Title: Apollo 13 - Service Module oxygen tank 2 failure
Date: 13 April 1970
Vehicle: Apollo 13 Command/Service Module
Sources: Report of the Apollo 13 Review Board (1970)

FAILURE MODE
Ignition of damaged electrical wiring insulation inside cryogenic oxygen tank 2, leading to overpressure and failure of the tank, loss of the service module's oxygen supply and loss of fuel-cell electrical power.

CAUSAL CHAIN
Before flight, the tank had been dropped about two inches during handling, which probably loosened its fill tube. When the tank could not be drained normally after a countdown test, its internal heaters were left on for hours to boil off the oxygen. The heaters' thermostatic protection switches were rated for the spacecraft's 28 V DC supply but not for the 65 V DC ground supply; they welded closed, and temperatures in the heater tube may have reached about 1,000 deg F, severely damaging the Teflon insulation on the fan and heater wiring. In flight, about 56 hours after launch, the crew switched on the tank's fans for a cryogenic stir. Damaged wiring short-circuited and ignited the insulation. Combustion raised tank pressure until the tank failed, which also caused oxygen tank 1 to leak.

TELEMETRY SIGNATURES
- Hours earlier, the tank 2 quantity gauge failed off-scale high.
- Electrical current transients when the fans were switched on.
- A rapid rise in tank 2 pressure and temperature over tens of seconds, then pressure falling to zero.
- A telemetry dropout of about two seconds.
- Main bus B undervoltage, followed by fuel-cell failures as their oxygen supply was lost; tank 1 pressure slowly decaying.

ROOT CAUSE
Electrical short and insulation fire inside the oxygen tank, enabled by heater switch incompatibility and ground-processing damage.

LESSON FOR ANOMALY ANALYSIS
An electrical event can initiate a pressure-vessel failure: check for current or voltage transients just before a pressure excursion, and remember that bus undervoltage may be a consequence rather than the cause.
