Title: Incomplete frangible joint or separation nut release
Category: separation
Failure mode: Incomplete frangible joint / separation nut release
Source type: synthetic
Case ID: sep-10-frangible-joint-separation-nut-release-failure
Note: Synthetic engineering summary of a failure mode, not a record of a specific event.

FAILURE MODE
Frangible joints use an expanding tube or a linear charge to fracture a notched structural ring along its whole circumference. Separation nuts are segmented nuts that release a bolt when fired. Both are used for stage, fairing and payload separation. An incomplete release leaves a section of ring unbroken or a bolt still held, keeping the bodies mechanically linked even though the ordnance fired.

CAUSAL CHAIN
The fire command is issued and firing current is nominal, so the pyrotechnic chain appears healthy. The first deviation is mechanical: a segment of the joint does not fully fracture, or a nut segment binds. Separation switches near the held point stay closed while others open. Bodies then either stay attached, so the vehicle continues with unexpected mass and a misaligned load path, or tear free late with a large tip-off, producing a sudden attitude rate on the free body.

TELEMETRY SIGNATURES
- Nominal firing current and initiator status, yet one sector's separation switches or breakwires remain intact
- Pyroshock amplitude lower than qualification levels on accelerometers near the held sector
- Delayed tear-out transient followed by attitude rate jump, or continued coupled motion of the two bodies
- Acceleration lower than predicted when the lower body stays attached

ROOT CAUSE
Typical causes are joint material outside specification (strength, ductility or wall thickness at the notch), a charge with reduced loading or a gap in the expanding tube, corrosion or contamination in a nut, preload above design, and assembly errors such as an incorrectly seated tube. Lot acceptance testing that does not reproduce flight temperatures can miss marginal hardware.

HISTORICAL REFERENCE
No single public incident is cited for this summary; it describes a generic failure mode recognised in launch-vehicle engineering practice. Fairing joint failures discussed in a separate summary show how material properties can affect release.

LESSONS LEARNED
- Control and test joint material properties per lot, especially at the notch
- Place separation indicators around the full circumference so a held sector is located
- Nominal firing current does not prove separation; check the mechanical indicators
