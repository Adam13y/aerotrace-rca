Title: Falcon 9 CRS-7 - second-stage LOX tank overpressure from a failed helium bottle strut
Date: 28 June 2015
Vehicle: SpaceX Falcon 9 (CRS-7 cargo mission)
Sources: SpaceX CRS-7 investigation update (July 2015); NASA Independent Review Team summary (2018)

FAILURE MODE
Overpressure and burst of the second-stage liquid oxygen (LOX) tank after a high-pressure helium bottle inside the tank broke loose, about 139 seconds into flight, while the first stage was still firing.

CAUSAL CHAIN
Helium pressurant for the second stage is stored in composite overwrapped pressure vessels (COPVs) mounted inside the LOX tank with steel struts. Under the high axial acceleration of first-stage flight (around 3.2 g), the buoyant helium bottle pulls hard on its struts. SpaceX's investigation found that one strut failed at roughly 2,000 lbf, far below its 10,000 lbf rating. The bottle broke free, its plumbing failed, and high-pressure helium flooded the LOX tank faster than the relief system could vent it. The tank overpressurised and burst, and the vehicle broke up. From the first sign of trouble to loss of telemetry was under one second. NASA's review team agreed the strut was a credible cause and also cited the design decision to use an industrial-grade cast part without adequate screening or testing.

TELEMETRY SIGNATURES
- A sudden pressure drop in the helium pressurisation system.
- At the same moment, a rapid rise in second-stage LOX tank ullage pressure: opposite-sign changes in a pressurant source and the tank it feeds.
- An event during powered flight near peak acceleration, not at a planned transient.
- An extremely short timeline (well under one second) before loss of signal.

ROOT CAUSE
Structural failure of a COPV support strut under flight loads, releasing helium into the LOX tank.

LESSON FOR ANOMALY ANALYSIS
Simultaneous opposite-sign pressure changes in connected volumes indicate an internal leak path. Structural failures of internal hardware tend to occur at peak load (high g), so check the acceleration profile at the anomaly time.
