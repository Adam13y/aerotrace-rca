Title: Structural overload near maximum dynamic pressure (Max-Q)
Category: aerodynamic
Failure mode: Max-Q structural loading
Source type: synthetic
Case ID: aero-01-max-q-structural-loading
Note: Synthetic engineering summary of a failure mode, not a record of a specific event.

FAILURE MODE
Maximum dynamic pressure (Max-Q) is the point of ascent where the product of air density and velocity squared peaks, typically about one minute after liftoff near Mach 1 to 1.5, at values of a few tens of kilopascals. Bending loads scale with dynamic pressure times angle of attack (q-alpha), so a small angle of attack near Max-Q produces large lateral loads on the vehicle, interstages and payload fairing.

CAUSAL CHAIN
The initiating deviation is usually a q-alpha larger than design: angle of attack grows because of winds, a trajectory or throttle error that raises dynamic pressure, or an engine-out that requires steering. Dynamic pressure or angle-of-attack estimates move outside their envelope first. Bending strain in the interstage and tank walls then rises within a fraction of a second. If the load exceeds capability, a joint or skin buckles; strain gauges saturate or drop out, tank pressure may fall, and attitude rates diverge within one or two seconds as the structure loses stiffness.

TELEMETRY SIGNATURES
- Dynamic pressure or q-alpha estimate exceeding the design envelope near Max-Q, roughly 50-80 s after liftoff on many vehicles
- Angle-of-attack or sideslip estimate growing before strain increases
- Bending strain gauges on interstage or tank walls rising sharply, then saturating or dropping out
- Tank pressure drop and attitude rate divergence following the strain peak by fractions of a second to seconds

ROOT CAUSE
Typical root causes are winds aloft outside the design envelope or not measured close to launch time, insufficient throttle-down for Max-Q, load-relief logic that is mistuned or disabled, structural margins eroded by mass growth or manufacturing defects, and errors in aerodynamic coefficients.

HISTORICAL REFERENCE
No single public incident is cited for this summary; it describes a generic failure mode recognised in launch-vehicle engineering practice.

LESSONS LEARNED
- Use day-of-launch wind measurements to check q-alpha against structural limits
- Tune load-relief and throttle-down so margins hold for dispersed winds and trajectories
- When strain and attitude anomalies cluster at Max-Q, check whether angle of attack deviated first
