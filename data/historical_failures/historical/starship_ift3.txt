Title: Starship Integrated Flight Test 3 - booster landing burn failure and ship loss during re-entry
Category: propulsion
Failure mode: Booster engine shutdowns during return burns; ship roll control loss before re-entry
Source type: historical
Case ID: starship_ift3
Date: 14 March 2024
Vehicle: Starship / Super Heavy (Ship 28, Booster 10)
Sources: the launch provider's Starship Flight 3 summary (2024); FAA statements (2024)

FAILURE MODE
The third integrated flight achieved a full ascent and hot-stage separation, and the Ship reached its planned coast trajectory for the first time. Both stages were then lost: the Super Heavy booster had engine shutdowns during its return burns and was lost before splashdown in the Gulf of Mexico, and the Ship was lost during re-entry after it was unable to control its roll attitude.

CAUSAL CHAIN
After hot staging, the booster performed its boostback burn, during which some engines shut down early. During the landing burn attempt, the booster did not achieve the planned engine operation and was lost a few hundred metres above the water. The launch provider's post-flight summary attributed the booster engine problems most likely to blockage in the oxidizer supply to the engines, a theme similar to Flight 2; the exact mechanism is less certain in public sources. The Ship completed its ascent burn and coasted, performing a payload door test and an in-flight propellant transfer demonstration. During the coast it developed roll rates higher than expected, and the planned in-space Raptor relight was skipped. The launch provider attributed the roll control problem most likely to clogging of valves used for roll control. Without proper attitude control, the Ship entered the atmosphere in an off-nominal orientation, experienced higher-than-planned heating, and signal was lost roughly 49 minutes into flight, well before the planned splashdown in the Indian Ocean. The FAA oversaw the provider-led mishap investigation and accepted its findings.

TELEMETRY SIGNATURES
- Booster: engine shutdowns during the boostback burn, followed by an incomplete or failed landing burn and loss of signal at low altitude.
- Booster: oxidizer inlet pressure anomalies on multiple engines, a shared-feed signature (less certain detail in public data).
- Ship: growing roll rate during coast despite control commands, with attitude-control valve or thruster performance below expectation.
- Ship: cancellation of a planned engine relight due to unsuitable attitude conditions.
- Ship: off-nominal attitude during re-entry, rising heating indications and loss of signal well before planned splashdown.

ROOT CAUSE
The launch provider identified clogging of roll control valves as the most likely cause of the Ship's attitude control loss, leading to an uncontrolled re-entry; reported corrective actions included adding roll control thrusters and improving resilience to clogging. For the booster, the launch provider attributed the engine shutdowns most likely to oxidizer supply blockage and reported hardware improvements to filtration; this detail is less certain than the Ship finding.

LESSONS LEARNED
- A flight can fail in independent ways on each stage; build separate timelines before searching for common causes.
- A control rate that grows while commands saturate indicates actuator or valve degradation, not a guidance fault.
- Attitude control problems in coast directly raise re-entry thermal risk; flag them as precursors.
- Recurring feed-system blockages across flights suggest a systemic contamination or design issue.
